## Context

What forces this decision now. The constraints, the specifications that apply,
what breaks if nothing is decided. Facts, not preferences.

State explicitly whether the decision was already constrained by an authoritative
document. A pre-locked decision is still worth an ADR, but the reader must know
they are reading rationale rather than deliberation.

## Decision

What is decided, in the active voice. One paragraph. If it needs three, the ADR
covers more than one decision — split it.

## Alternatives considered

For each: what it is, why it was plausible, why it was not chosen. An ADR with no
credible alternative is not a decision, it is a note.

## Pros

## Cons

What this costs, concretely. **Be specific.** An ADR whose cons section is vague
is the one that gets reversed in eighteen months by someone who did not know what
they were inheriting.

## Future impact

- What becomes easy.
- What becomes hard.
- What would have to be true to revisit this.
- Estimated cost of reversal.
