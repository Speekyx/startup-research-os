## Why an anaphoric answer is not the presupposition route

a presupposition is something implied by a statement ABOUT SOMETHING ELSE -- Mission 1.76.1's no-proxy carve-out, which mentions proxying and leaves third-party scope to be reasoned out. An anaphoric answer is a statement about THE QUESTION: 'yes' asserts the question's proposition and nothing has to be inferred to reach it. Every one of the seven routes the discriminator excludes is a case of reasoning from adjacent material, and a direct answer is not one.

its exclusion list is the place such a requirement would live, and it names silence, a generic commercial permission, documentation phrasing, product behaviour, the proxy limitation alone, absence from a prohibited-use list, and implication or presupposition. 'An answer that does not restate the scope' is not among them.

**had the provider meant to refuse, this reply would not be the one they sent. Shortness is not vagueness: to a bounded yes-or-no question, 'Yes it's not a problem' has one reading.**
