If the queryable timestamp is a last-seen marker on a maintained record, a window filter selects services whose LATEST detection fell inside the window, and a service observed both before and after but not during it behaves differently from one genuinely observed inside it. That is the Mission 1.59 failure and the LeakIX failure.

## The ambiguity carried in

> timestamp of when the data was collected. Allows tracking when a given service or vulnerability was last observed.

The first clause describes a collection moment, which is an observation event. The second describes tracking when a service was LAST observed, which is what a maintained record's advancing timestamp does. One sentence, two temporal objects.

## What the query-language page adds

> These functions allows you to search through historical data.

| function | documented bucket boundary |
| --- | --- |
| `-hourago` | an hour starts at minute 00 and ends at minute 59 |
| `-dayago` | a day starts at 00:00 hour and ends at 23:59 hour |
| `-weekago` | a week starts on Monday at 00:00 and ends on Sunday at 23:59 |
| `-monthago` | a month starts the 1st at 00:00 and ends last day of the month at 23:59 |

*Establishes:* Historical search exists, and the buckets are calendar-aligned rather than rolling. A window is expressible in the request.

*Does not establish:* What the filtered timestamp MEANS at record level. Calendar-aligned buckets over a last-seen field are still buckets over a last-seen field.

*Granularity bound:* The documented selectors are RELATIVE bucket offsets rather than an absolute half-open interval. A frozen calendar window would have to be expressed at retrieval time as an offset computed from the retrieval date. That is deterministic and it couples the request's wording to when it is sent.

## The diagnostic

Suppose service S was observed both before and after window W, but not during W. Would the documented filter include or exclude it, and on what basis?

**`NOT_DETERMINABLE_FROM_DOCUMENTATION`.** The functions are documented as searching historical data over calendar buckets. Nothing states whether the value they filter is a per-observation collection time or a maintained record's latest sighting. Under the first reading S is excluded, correctly. Under the second, S is included or excluded according to where its latest detection fell, which is a different question.

## Verdict

`AMBIGUOUS` / B2 `PARTIAL`. The ambiguity is now bounded rather than open. Historical search is established, the bucket boundaries are established and calendar-aligned, and the exact missing fact is a single sentence about what the filtered timestamp means at record level.

**The interpretation that was not chosen.** The reading under which the timestamp is a per-observation collection time and B2 passes. It is the reading that preserves the candidate, and section 9 forbids choosing it for that reason.

*What would close it:* One first-party sentence stating either that each scan appends a record carrying its own collection time, or that a service record is updated in place as later scans observe it. Either answer closes the gate; one closes it as a PASS and one as a FAIL.
